$ shore excursion — included — Napoleon's villa, Portoferraio

Napoleon's villa is not big enough. That is the whole exhibit.

Portoferraio has been catching people who didn't entirely choose it since 1814. It caught us in the same spirit.

Horseshoe harbour. Old town stacked behind it in the standard Italian arrangement of laundry and geraniums. Above the lot, the villa — when a town has had exactly one famous resident, it does not waste him.

The included excursion went up the hill. The man had been running a continent the previous spring. Here was a house you could walk end to end in the time it takes to lose a tour group, gardens included.

Plus a terrace from which he could inspect his entire jurisdiction without binoculars.

We did the rooms at the pace the group allowed, which is the pace of the slowest photographer. The rooms held their own. Small windows, sea light, furniture built for a man planning to be elsewhere.

He kept the full ceremony going while he was here. The household, the receptions, the etiquette of the Tuileries — transplanted onto an audience of one small island. Reads either as dignity or as a man declining to update his org chart.

::drop Three hundred days. He rebuilt the roads, reorganised the iron mines, drafted a constitution — on an island he could see the whole of from his terrace. So what would we get done with three hundred days and no meetings?

::drop He gave the island a flag before he gave it anything else. White, one red stripe, three golden bees. What does a man fix on day one, and what does that tell you?

The afternoon was ours once the villa was done. Elba in the afternoon is the kind of luxury it would be indelicate to describe to people back home.

We sailed the same evening. An exit it took him three hundred days to organise.
