::map palermo

The ceiling switched itself on. Acres of gold mosaic, depending on whose survey you trust. Every surface employed.

That was Monreale, Wednesday, up the hill before the heat had properly reported for duty — the only hour that town receives visitors on fair terms. William II ran the cathedral up in the 1170s, largely to annoy the Archbishop of Palermo. You come in out of the white glare of the square and the whole thing reads like a ledger that only ever recorded credits.

::drop Somewhere in the nave William hands the finished church up to the Virgin. He is holding the entire building in his two hands, like a model. What would you build, at twenty-two, if nobody could stop you?

::prompt What did Claire say inside Monreale?

Then back down into Ballarò. Full voice by mid-morning — the market's only setting. We let it carry us through, on the theory that the last morning is the one where you finally buy the things you have been circling all week.

::prompt What came home from Ballarò?

The afternoon was assigned to the Villa Igiea pool. I want it minuted that we endured it. The water held its temperature without apology, and the sea lay flat beyond the balustrade, doing nothing to help.

Hour after hour, we bore it. Nobody packed so much as a sock — the flight wasn't until Thursday midday, and a bag half-zipped on a Wednesday is a sign of weakness.

Conditions, in short, remained superb to the end.

There was one last dinner. The grand kind, the sort Sicily had been auditioning for all fortnight. It went long, as the last one is contractually required to.
