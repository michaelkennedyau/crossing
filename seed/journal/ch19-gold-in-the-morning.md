Wednesday we went up the hill to Monreale before the heat had properly reported for duty, which is the only hour that town receives visitors on fair terms, and stood in the cathedral William II ran up in the 1170s largely to annoy the Archbishop of Palermo, acres of gold mosaic depending on whose survey you trust, every surface employed, and you come in out of the white glare of the square and the whole ceiling switches itself on above you like a ledger that only ever recorded credits.

::drop Somewhere in the nave William is handing the finished church up to the Virgin, holding the entire building in his two hands like a model. What would you build, at twenty-two, if nobody could stop you?

::prompt What did Claire say inside Monreale?

Then back down into Palermo and into Ballarò, which by mid-morning was at full voice, which is the market's only setting, and we let it carry us through on the theory that the last morning is the one where you finally buy the things you have been circling all week.

::prompt What came home from Ballarò?

The afternoon was assigned to the Villa Igiea pool and I want it minuted that we endured it, the water holding its temperature without apology, the sea lying flat on the other side of the balustrade doing nothing to help, and hour after hour we bore it, because nobody packed so much as a sock, the flight not being until Thursday midday, and a bag half-zipped on a Wednesday is a sign of weakness.

Conditions, in short, remained grim to the end.

There was one last dinner, the grand kind, the sort Sicily had been auditioning for all fortnight, and it went long, as the last one is contractually required to.
