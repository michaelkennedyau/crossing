The St John Paul II left Valletta at 07:30, and Virtu are firm on the point that she is the largest high-speed catamaran in the Mediterranean, which is the sort of fact a boat leads with when it would rather you thought about size than movement. An hour and three quarters of open water between Malta and Sicily, most of it before nine in the morning, and Pozzallo at 09:15, which as ports go is less a destination than a thing ferries admit to on the way past.

The land side had gone to the trade channel, one car, one driver, 230 kilometres from the bottom-right corner of the island to the top-left of it, and the quotes came back between €280 and €380, which is a hundred euros of daylight on the same road with the same amount of Sicily in the middle of it.

Whoever won carried us through the interior, which is the part of the island the postcards skip, the wheat and the distance and the towns that sit up on their hills and watch the road go by without much interest, and by early afternoon we were at Villa Igiea, having started the day in one country and finished the drive in another with a sea in between and no airport involved, which is the correct way to do it and almost never the way it gets done.
