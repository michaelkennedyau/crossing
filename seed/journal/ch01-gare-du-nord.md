One minute. That is what three men and a lanyard cost us.

The 13:31 out of St Pancras did what it always does: made France feel like a suburb. At 16:49 we hit Gare du Nord with eighty kilos of luggage and seventy minutes to cross town for the 17:56 to Lyon.

At the métro barriers a man wearing a lanyard explained that he worked there. He did not. Two colleagues of his arranged themselves behind us.

There followed a short conversation, in two languages, about whether our tickets and bags required his assistance. They did not. Claire was behind me the whole time.

The tickets stayed where they were. The three gentlemen went off to find easier company. We missed the 17:56 — the part I am still filing paperwork about.

The pré-plainte went in online that evening. Four attachments. The French state received our account with the administrative warmth of a toll booth.

The 19:00 took us to Lyon instead — a city neither of us had planned to sleep in. The kind of navigational error you would pay money for. In a sense, we had.
