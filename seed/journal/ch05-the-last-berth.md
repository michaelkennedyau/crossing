The water had no business being that colour.

Last morning ashore, so we spent it at the beach. Claire progressed the tan. I conducted a full review of the sand — ordinary sand, doing ordinary sand things.

Conditions overall remained grim. Sun, salt, nothing required of anyone until four.

The ship had been booked two days out, on one of the last berths going. Not the recommended run-up to ten nights at sea. Exactly the run-up we used.

Two days is roughly the window between finding out a thing exists and watching somebody else get it. We have run that analysis before. The decision took about as long as it deserved.

::doctrine "When the right asset shows up late, the answer is still yes."

::ledger 23,280 AUD — the ship, ten nights, all of it

$ Le Dumont d'Urville · boarding 16:00 · sail 18:00 · Cabin 306, Deck 3

At 16:00 we boarded Le Dumont d'Urville. 184 passengers, 92 staterooms, 118 crew — better than one crew member for every two of us. I checked the ratio twice and elected not to argue with it.

Cabin 306, Deck 3. The balcony sat close enough to the water that the water stopped being a view — it became a fitting of the room, part of the joinery, present at all hours.

::prompt When did we know 306 was the right cabin?

::ledger 400 EUR — Virtuoso credit, to be spent aboard, fate unrecorded

::prompt What did the €400 Virtuoso credit become?

She sailed at 18:00. Nice went off the stern slowly, the beach we had audited that morning included.

The arithmetic was pleasing. Morning on the sand, evening at sea, nothing owed anywhere in between. Ten nights of this lay ahead, and I want it recorded that we faced them without complaint.
