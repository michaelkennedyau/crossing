The last morning ashore went to the beach, where Claire progressed the tan and I conducted a full review of the sand, which was ordinary sand, doing ordinary sand things, in front of water that had no business being that colour, and conditions overall remained grim, sun, salt, nothing required of anyone until four.

The ship had been booked two days out, on one of the last berths going, which is not the recommended run-up to ten nights at sea and is exactly the run-up we used. Two days is roughly the window between finding out a thing exists and watching somebody else get it, and we have run that analysis before, so the decision took about as long as it deserved to.

::doctrine "When the right asset shows up late, the answer is still yes."

::ledger 23,280 AUD — the ship, ten nights, all of it

$ Le Dumont d'Urville · boarding 16:00 · sail 18:00 · Cabin 306, Deck 3

At 16:00 we boarded Le Dumont d'Urville, 184 passengers, 92 staterooms, 118 crew, which works out to better than one crew member for every two of us, a ratio I checked twice and elected not to argue with. Cabin 306, Deck 3, which put the balcony close enough to the water that the water stopped being a view and became a fitting of the room, part of the joinery, present at all hours.

She sailed at 18:00 and Nice went off the stern slowly, the beach we had audited that morning included, and the arithmetic of it was pleasing, morning on the sand, evening at sea, nothing owed anywhere in between. Ten nights of this lay ahead, and I want it recorded that we faced them without complaint.
