We sailed out of Porto Ercole overnight and woke to the first day of the whole run with no port on it, no tender times, no meeting point, no man with a clipboard, just water in every direction and the day standing there empty, asking what I intended to do about it.

So the ship stopped being the thing that carried us between places and became the place itself, the same decks in the same rotation, the sea going past at a speed nobody could verify, no plan to execute and no way to fall behind one, which for a man who runs on a schedule was a set of conditions I can only describe as grim.

::drop The wake was the only evidence we were getting anywhere. How fast does a ship need to go before you stop checking?

::prompt What actually filled the hours between breakfast and the horizon watch?

Somewhere in the middle of all that nothing it occurred to me that this was the last piece of pure infrastructure on the whole trip, steel and timetables and staff whose lanyards mean what they say, because from Sicily on the thing was being handed over to people, and people, unlike ships, do not publish schedules.

::doctrine "The pause is part of the plan."

A day with nothing in it still gets a card in this family, and Claire's card is hers to write, so I kept my speculation to myself, which is not the same as not having any.

::prompt What was Claire's star for the sea day?

By evening we were at the rail for the horizon watch, the Aeolians somewhere ahead in the dark, and off to the south there may have been a thin line of smoke standing up off the water where Stromboli keeps its fire going, or it may have been cloud doing a reasonable impression of one, the light was going and I have declined to rule on it.

::drop A mountain that has been burning since before there were countries, filed by the shipping lanes under navigation aids. What else out there works that hard to be taken for granted?

::prompt Did Stromboli smoke?
