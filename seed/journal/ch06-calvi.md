::map calvi

Walls first. Out of the water, straight up, until they filled every window on the boat.

First tender of the trip. The ship anchors off. Everyone files down to sea level and goes ashore in a boat the size of a bus. The whole arrangement exists so the citadel can do its trick. Whoever put those walls there wanted them judged from exactly where we were sitting, and I can report the judging went well.

::prompt What did Claire photograph from the tender?

Ashore, the included walking tour. Included in the sense that we had already paid for it and were therefore going. A guide took us up into the citadel on cobbles worn somewhere past marble, under a sun also included at no additional cost.

Conditions, in short, remained grim. We soldiered up through the gateways with the rest of the tender, single file where the walls insisted on it.

::prompt What did the guide say that survived the day?

::drop Nelson lost the sight of his right eye besieging these walls in 1794. We had the same walls in front of us and both eyes working, so what would he have called a fair price for the view?

Below the ramparts the water was doing what Corsican water is contracted to do. That raised the only real question of the afternoon.

The fork in the day was the Revellata snorkel. €150 a head, optional — and €150 a head is where optional stops being a word and becomes a position. One side of the fork: the clearest water of the itinerary, with a guide in it. The other: the same water, admired from land, at no charge.

The case for each side was heard in full. On cobbles. In the sun.
