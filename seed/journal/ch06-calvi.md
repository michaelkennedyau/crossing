Calvi was the first tender of the trip, tendering being the system where the ship anchors off, everyone files down to sea level, and you go ashore in a boat the size of a bus, and the whole arrangement exists mainly so the citadel can do its trick, which is come up out of the water walls-first and keep coming until it has filled every window on the boat. Whoever put those walls there wanted them judged from exactly where we were sitting, and I can report the judging went well.

::prompt What did Claire photograph from the tender?

Ashore, the included walking tour, included in the sense that we had already paid for it and were therefore going, and a guide took us up into the citadel on cobbles worn somewhere past marble, under a sun that was also included at no additional cost. Conditions, in short, remained grim, and we soldiered up through the gateways with the rest of the tender, single file where the walls insisted on it.

::prompt What did the guide say that survived the day?

::drop Nelson lost the sight of his right eye besieging these walls in 1794. We had the same walls in front of us and both eyes working, so what would he have called a fair price for the view?

Below the ramparts the water was doing what Corsican water is contracted to do, which raised the only real question of the afternoon.

The fork in the day was the Revellata snorkel, €150 a head and optional, and €150 a head is the point where optional stops being a word and becomes a position, because on one side of the fork was the clearest water of the itinerary with a guide in it, and on the other was the same water, admired from land, at no charge, and the case for each side was heard in full, on cobbles, in the sun.
