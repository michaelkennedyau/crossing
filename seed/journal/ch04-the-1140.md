The 11:40 went south, which is all we asked of it, and for once the plan and the timetable were the same document. One change at Marseille, a phrase that reads harmlessly enough on a booking confirmation and involves rather more luggage in person, and we made the second train with time to spare, which after Paris felt like showing off.

$ TKD3G6 / BL4P7Q / HPW6G9 — three references, two trains, zero negotiations at any barrier.

Out of Marseille the sea turned up on the right-hand side and stayed there the rest of the way, the whole coast running along beside the train like it had nothing better to do, and nobody charges extra for that yet, though give them time.

Hotel Suisse is on Quai Rauba Capeù, which is Niçois rather than French and which nobody needs to pronounce correctly to be let in. The lift was small, the room was not, and when I opened the shutters the entire Baie des Anges was in the window, the full bay, blue out to the horizon, at which point I noted for the record that conditions had deteriorated further and were now frankly unreasonable.

The seaview was itemised on the invoice, which is correct procedure, hardship at that level should always be receipted, and breakfast was included, so there was no prospect of the situation improving before mid-morning at the earliest.

We had come a long way for water and the water had turned up in bulk, on schedule, directly below the window. Somewhere down the hill that evening there was a first dinner in Nice, and that is exactly where the record goes thin.
