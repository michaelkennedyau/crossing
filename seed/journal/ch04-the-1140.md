The 11:40 went south. That is all we asked of it. For once the plan and the timetable were the same document.

One change at Marseille. Reads harmlessly on a booking confirmation; involves rather more luggage in person. We made the second train with time to spare, which after Paris felt like showing off.

::prompt What did Marseille look like from the platform?

$ TKD3G6 / BL4P7Q / HPW6G9 — three references, two trains, zero negotiations at any barrier.

Out of Marseille the sea turned up on the right-hand side and stayed there the rest of the way. The whole coast ran alongside the train like it had nothing better to do. Nobody charges extra for that yet, though give them time.

Hotel Suisse is on Quai Rauba Capeù — Niçois rather than French, and nobody needs to pronounce it correctly to be let in. The lift was small. The room was not.

Then I opened the shutters. The entire Baie des Anges was in the window — the full bay, blue out to the horizon. I noted for the record that conditions had deteriorated further and were now frankly unreasonable.

::prompt What could you see when the shutters opened?

::ledger 880 AUD — Hotel Suisse, the seaview, breakfast

The seaview was itemised on the invoice. Correct procedure — luxury at that level should always be receipted. Breakfast was included, so the standard was in no danger before mid-morning at the earliest.

We had come a long way for water. The water turned up in bulk, on schedule, directly below the window.

Somewhere down the hill that evening there was a first dinner in Nice. That is exactly where the record goes thin.
