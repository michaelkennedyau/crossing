Mdina calls itself the Silent City. I audited the claim. It held up.

A walled town on a hill that stopped mattering around 1565 and has declined to comment since. Streets a horse-width across. Limestone the colour of the hour. Door knockers above eye level, for people who no longer answer.

Every other place on this trip had spent the day explaining itself at us. Here was a whole city with nothing to add. We walked it at the pace it asked for and added nothing either.

::prompt What did Mdina sound like?

Back at the Grand Harbour we crossed to the Three Cities by dghajsa — a gondola with a proper job. You sit at water level in a painted boat, on a channel that has moved somebody's cargo since before the Phoenicians invented the receipt.

The Knights ran their fleet out of these creeks. Then the Royal Navy. Now the same water carries tourists at the same unhurried angle. As career changes go, not the worst.

::prompt What did the dghajsa boatman talk about?

::drop Maltese is the last living branch of Siculo-Arabic, and the most Catholic country in Europe says its prayers to Alla. What does a language keep that its speakers have agreed not to mention?

Then out along the coast to St Peter's Pool. Not a pool. A shelf of pale rock the sea has cut into the shape of a decision.

Flat stone, clear water, a drop of a few metres. The place asks everyone on the edge exactly one question. It is a very old question, and the rock is neutral — it has watched people answer both ways for as long as there have been feet.

We stood there a while, the water doing its colours below us.
