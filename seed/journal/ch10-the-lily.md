::map giglio

Granita at the port. That is the morning's one hard fact.

Giglio does its mornings at the harbour — a curve of coloured houses around the boats, not much else. An island that size has already decided where you'll be standing. We didn't argue.

The rest of the detail has gone wherever mornings go.

::prompt Where was the granita and what flavour?

Giannutri sat out there the whole time. A smaller island, an hour off the bow, the open question. We had done the deciding in advance — decided how we would decide, then declined to reopen the file. Per the rules.

::doctrine "Rule 5: the switch criterion is decided at the fork, once."

::prompt Did the Giannutri boat happen?

Either way, the water off Giglio Porto runs past Le Scole. A low line of rocks just south of the harbour mouth. Every boat that comes or goes passes them.

::drop Le Scole is where the Costa Concordia went onto the rocks on the night of 13 January 2012. Thirty-two people died. Taking the ship off the island afterwards became the largest maritime salvage in history.

::prompt What was said, if anything, passing Le Scole?

The rocks are just rocks again. The water over them is the same water as everywhere else on that coast. That's the island's answer to the whole business, and probably the right one.

They call Giglio the lily. Apparently the name comes from an old Greek word to do with goats, not the flower. The island has clearly decided not to pursue the matter — by mid-morning that struck us as sound policy.
