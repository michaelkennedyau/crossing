::map giglio

Giglio does its mornings at the port, which is a curve of coloured houses arranged around the boats and not much else, and we did ours there too, on the theory that an island that size has already decided where you will be standing and there is no point arguing with it.

There was a granita, which is the one hard fact the morning left behind, and the rest of the detail has gone wherever mornings go.

::prompt Where was the granita and what flavour?

Giannutri sat out there the whole time as the open question, a smaller island an hour off the bow, and we had done the deciding about it in advance, which is to say we had decided how we would decide and then declined to reopen the file, per the rules.

::doctrine "Rule 5: the switch criterion is decided at the fork, once."

::prompt Did the Giannutri boat happen?

Either way, the water off Giglio Porto runs past Le Scole, a low line of rocks just south of the harbour mouth, and every boat that comes or goes passes them.

::drop Le Scole is where the Costa Concordia went onto the rocks on the night of 13 January 2012. Thirty-two people died, and taking the ship off the island afterwards became the largest maritime salvage in history.

::prompt What was said, if anything, passing Le Scole?

The rocks are just rocks again now, and the water over them is the same water as everywhere else on that coast, which is the island's answer to the whole business and probably the right one.

They call Giglio the lily, though apparently the name comes from an old Greek word to do with goats rather than the flower, and the island has clearly decided not to pursue the matter, which by mid-morning struck us as sound policy.
