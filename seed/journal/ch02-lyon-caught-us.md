::map lyon

We woke up in a city neither of us had chosen. That is an unusual way to meet one. Nothing promised, so nothing owed.

The hotel used to be the Hôtel Dieu, a hospital from the seventeenth century, and they kept the bones. Corridors wide enough for stretchers. A dome built for last rites, now dispensing breakfast — same mission of making people feel better, revised pricing.

By mid-morning Lyon was baking properly, the stone handing the heat back with interest. We retreated into the Halles Paul Bocuse: a city that decided its market deserved a roof and its greatest cook's name over the door. We laid in provisions with the seriousness of people who had no plan to serve them.

::prompt What came home from Halles Paul Bocuse and what got eaten standing up?

Towards evening we took la ficelle up to Fourvière. The funicular. Two minutes of gradient the city stopped noticing sometime last century.

::drop The string has been hauling Lyon up its own hill since 1862, and the town is so unimpressed it named it after kitchen twine. What does a city have to see before a hundred and sixty years of not falling counts as showing off?

At the top: terraces, the whole city laid out under them, both rivers, the roofs going pink. Also a bottle of champagne that was not going to survive another day of that heat. It had to be dealt with on the spot, and we would like it minuted that we objected.

Sunset was 20:46 and it ran on time — more than can be said for the service that delivered us here in the first place. Conditions, in short, remained grim.

Somewhere between the market and the bottle it was settled: we were not leaving in the morning. Nobody remembers a vote being held.
