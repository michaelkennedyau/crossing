::map lyon

We woke up in a city neither of us had chosen, which is an unusual way to meet one, nothing promised so nothing owed. The hotel used to be the Hôtel Dieu, a hospital from the seventeenth century, and they have kept the bones of it, corridors wide enough for stretchers, a dome built for last rites, and the building now dispenses breakfast instead, same mission of making people feel better, revised pricing.

By mid-morning Lyon was baking, properly, the stone handing the heat back with interest, so we retreated into the Halles Paul Bocuse, which is what happens when a city decides its market deserves a roof and its greatest cook's name over the door, and we laid in provisions with the seriousness of people who had no plan to serve them.

::prompt What came home from Halles Paul Bocuse and what got eaten standing up?

Towards evening we took la ficelle up to Fourvière, the funicular, two minutes of gradient the city stopped noticing sometime last century.

::drop The string has been hauling Lyon up its own hill since 1862, and the town is so unimpressed it named it after kitchen twine. What does a city have to see before a hundred and sixty years of not falling counts as showing off?

At the top there are terraces with the whole city laid out under them, both rivers, the roofs going pink, and we had a bottle of champagne that was not going to survive another day of that heat, so it had to be dealt with on the spot, and we would like it minuted that we objected. Sunset was 20:46 and it ran on time, which is more than can be said for the service that delivered us here in the first place. Conditions, in short, remained grim.

Somewhere between the market and the bottle it was settled that we were not leaving in the morning, although nobody remembers a vote being held.
